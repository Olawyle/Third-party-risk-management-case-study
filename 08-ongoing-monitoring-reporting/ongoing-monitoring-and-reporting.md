# Stage 8 — Ongoing Monitoring & Reporting

## 1. Objective

Establish an ongoing monitoring approach to ensure CloudServe Technologies Ltd.'s risk posture remains aligned with FinServe's requirements throughout the vendor relationship.

Approval is not considered the end of the TPRM process. Monitoring provides continued visibility into changes that may affect the vendor's risk profile.

---

## 2. Monitoring Approach

Monitoring will be risk-based and proportionate to the vendor's **Tier 1 — Critical** classification.

The monitoring program will focus on:

- Security and control performance
- Open remediation activities
- Security incidents and breaches
- Changes to critical services
- Material changes in ownership or operations
- Fourth-party dependencies
- Regulatory or contractual changes
- Independent assurance and certification status
- Business continuity and disaster recovery performance

---

## 3. Monitoring Schedule

| Monitoring Activity | Frequency | Responsible Function |
|---|---|---|
| Critical remediation tracking | Monthly | GRC / TPRM |
| Security incidents and material events | Continuous / Event-driven | Information Security |
| Vendor risk indicators | Quarterly | GRC / TPRM |
| Security assurance and certifications | Semi-annual | GRC / Information Security |
| Fourth-party risk review | Semi-annual | TPRM / Vendor Management |
| Business continuity / DR performance | Annual / Event-driven | Business Continuity / TPRM |
| Comprehensive vendor reassessment | Annual | GRC / TPRM |

Monitoring frequency may be increased where material risk events or significant control weaknesses are identified.

---

## 4. Trigger Events for Reassessment

A reassessment should be initiated when a material change occurs, including:

- Significant security incident or data breach
- Material change in the vendor's ownership or organizational structure
- Introduction of a new critical service or technology
- Significant change in data processing or access privileges
- Material change to fourth-party dependencies
- Loss or expiry of relevant security certifications
- Significant regulatory or contractual change
- Repeated failure to meet remediation commitments
- Material deterioration in the vendor's security posture
- Significant business continuity or service-performance failure

---

## 5. Risk Monitoring Indicators

The following indicators will support ongoing oversight:

| Indicator | Expected Condition | Escalation Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| High-severity vulnerabilities | Remediated within agreed SLA | Overdue remediation |
| Security incidents | No material unresolved incidents | Material incident |
| Remediation actions | Progressing against agreed dates | Repeated delays |
| Security assurance | Current and valid | Expired / unavailable |
| DR testing | Required RTO achieved | Repeated RTO failure |
| Fourth-party assurance | Current for critical dependencies | Overdue assessment |
| Contractual requirements | Current and compliant | Material gap identified |

These indicators provide ongoing signals rather than relying solely on an annual point-in-time assessment.

---

## 6. Reporting & Escalation

Monitoring results should be reported through the organization's established vendor-risk governance process.

Reporting should include:

- Current vendor risk rating
- Open and overdue findings
- Remediation progress
- Material security events
- Changes to the vendor risk profile
- Fourth-party risk developments
- Assurance and certification status
- Required management actions

Material deterioration in the vendor's risk posture should be escalated to the appropriate risk owner and may trigger reassessment or additional treatment.

---

## 7. Periodic Reassessment

The vendor should undergo a comprehensive reassessment at least annually, or sooner where a defined trigger event occurs.

The reassessment should determine whether:

- The vendor's criticality remains unchanged
- Existing controls remain effective
- Previously identified findings have been adequately remediated
- New risks have emerged
- Residual risk remains within the organization's tolerance
- The existing risk decision remains appropriate

---

## 8. Final TPRM Lifecycle Outcome

The TPRM lifecycle for CloudServe Technologies Ltd. progresses from:

**Vendor Profiling → Inherent Risk Assessment → Due Diligence → Control Assessment → Risk Treatment → Residual Risk Assessment → Risk Decision → Ongoing Monitoring**

The vendor remains **Tier 1 — Critical** and subject to enhanced monitoring.

The conditional approval established in Stage 7 remains dependent on effective remediation and continued risk oversight.

---

## 9. Stage 8 Outcome

**Vendor Status:** Conditionally Approved

**Vendor Tier:** Tier 1 — Critical

**Overall Residual Risk:** Medium

**Monitoring Level:** Enhanced

**Reassessment:** Annual or event-driven

**Remediation Tracking:** Ongoing

**Final TPRM Status:** Active — Enhanced Monitoring

The TPRM process is considered an ongoing lifecycle rather than a one-time assessment. Changes in the vendor's risk profile will trigger reassessment, additional treatment, escalation, or a review of the existing risk decision.
