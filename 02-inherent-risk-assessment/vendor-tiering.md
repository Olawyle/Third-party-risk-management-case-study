# Vendor Tiering Assessment

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of vendor tiering is to translate the inherent risk assessment into a practical level of third-party oversight and due diligence.

Vendor tiering helps FinServe Financial Services Ltd. apply a **risk-based approach** by allocating greater oversight and assurance requirements to vendors presenting higher levels of inherent risk.

---

## 2. Vendor Tiering Model

FinServe will use a three-tier model for this case study.

| Tier       | Risk Classification | Vendor Characteristics                                                                                     | Typical Treatment                              |
| ---------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Tier 1** | Critical / High     | Critical business dependency, sensitive data, significant access, major regulatory or operational exposure | Enhanced due diligence and ongoing monitoring  |
| **Tier 2** | Moderate            | Meaningful business or information exposure with manageable impact                                         | Standard due diligence and periodic monitoring |
| **Tier 3** | Low                 | Limited business dependency, minimal data exposure, limited access                                         | Simplified due diligence                       |

### Tiering Principle

The assigned tier should reflect the **overall risk and business significance of the vendor relationship**, rather than relying solely on a single characteristic.

The final tier should consider:

* Inherent risk score
* Business criticality
* Data sensitivity
* Data volume
* Access and integration
* Fourth-party dependency
* Regulatory exposure
* Concentration risk
* Availability of alternative providers

---

## 3. Tier 1 Criteria

A vendor should generally be considered **Tier 1** where one or more of the following conditions apply:

* Critical or high inherent risk
* Significant processing of sensitive or regulated information
* Large-scale processing of customer or employee information
* Significant dependency for critical business processes
* Privileged or material system access
* Integration with important organizational systems
* Significant fourth-party dependency
* Material regulatory or compliance exposure
* Significant operational, financial, or reputational impact if the service fails

Tier 1 vendors require enhanced governance and more extensive due diligence.

## 6. Tier 1 Due Diligence Requirements

As a Tier 1 vendor, CloudServe should undergo enhanced due diligence before final approval.

The assessment should include, at minimum:

### Governance & Security

* Information security governance
* Security policies
* Security organization and responsibilities
* Risk management practices
* Security awareness and training

### Independent Assurance

* SOC 2 Type II report, where available
* ISO/IEC 27001 certification, where available
* Independent penetration testing evidence
* Relevant audit reports
* Security assessment results

### Technical Security

* Identity and access management
* Privileged access management
* MFA
* Encryption
* Vulnerability management
* Patch management
* Security monitoring
* Logging
* Secure software development

### Data Protection

* Data inventory
* Data classification
* Data processing activities
* Data residency
* Cross-border transfers
* Retention and deletion
* Encryption
* Privacy controls

### Resilience

* Business continuity plan
* Disaster recovery plan
* RTO/RPO
* Backup strategy
* Recovery testing
* Service availability history

### Incident Management

* Incident response plan
* Breach notification process
* Escalation procedures
* Customer notification commitments
* Previous material incidents, where applicable

### Fourth-Party Risk

* Subprocessor inventory
* Critical subcontractor assessment
* Cloud provider assurance
* Fourth-party security requirements
* Material change notification

### Contractual Controls

* Security requirements
* Data protection obligations
* Audit rights
* Incident notification timelines
* Confidentiality requirements
* Business continuity obligations
* Data return and deletion
* Termination and exit provisions

## 7. Ongoing Monitoring Requirements

Because CloudServe is classified as Tier 1, ongoing monitoring should be more frequent than for lower-tier vendors.

Potential monitoring activities include:

* Annual comprehensive security reassessment
* Review of updated SOC 2 or ISO 27001 evidence
* Review of material security incidents
* Monitoring of significant changes to the service
* Subprocessor changes
* Changes to data processing locations
* Material changes in access or integrations
* Business continuity testing results
* Security rating or threat intelligence monitoring, where appropriate
* Tracking of outstanding risk issues

Monitoring frequency should ultimately be determined by FinServe's risk appetite and third-party risk policy.

---

## 8. Reassessment Triggers

CloudServe should be reassessed when significant changes occur, including:

* Major changes to the service
* Significant changes to data processed
* New system integrations
* New privileged access
* Material security incidents
* Significant changes to subcontractors
* Changes to hosting or data-processing locations
* Major regulatory changes
* Significant changes in business criticality
* Contract renewal or material contract amendments
